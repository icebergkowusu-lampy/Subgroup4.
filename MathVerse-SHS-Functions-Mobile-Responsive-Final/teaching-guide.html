TEACHING GUIDE

Pedagogy behind MathVerse

The philosophy, learning theory, competencies and citizenship principles that shaped this lesson — useful reference for facilitators and for the practicum presentation.

Teaching philosophy

Mathematics should not be presented as a collection of rules to memorise. Learners should actively construct mathematical understanding through exploration, discussion, problem solving and application.

The teacher's role is that of a facilitator: designing meaningful experiences, asking guiding questions, encouraging participation, and offering support where needed. Technology is used deliberately — sliders, live graphs and instant feedback — to make mathematics visible and interactive rather than abstract.

Learning theory

Constructivist Learning Theory guides the lesson design:

  1. Activate prior algebra knowledge before introducing new notation.
  2. Let learners investigate patterns in tables and graphs themselves.
  3. Use group discussion so learners test ideas against each other.
  4. Provide teacher guidance through questions, not direct answers.
  5. Close with structured reflection so learners consolidate understanding.

Core competencies (NaCCA)

Critical Thinking & Problem Solving
Communication & Collaboration
Digital Literacy
Creativity & Innovation

Assessment strategy

Assessment for learning

Questioning, observation, group activities and an in-lesson exit question, all with immediate feedback.

Assessment as learning

Self-check success criteria that learners revisit to judge their own progress.

Assessment of learning

A ten-question graded assessment with automatic scoring and a downloadable summary.

Digital citizenship

  • Acknowledge sources
  • Respect copyright
  • Use educational resources responsibly
  • Do not plagiarise
  • Use AI responsibly
  • Verify mathematical information
  • Protect personal information
  • Respect others online

AI tools can support learning — for example, explaining a step or suggesting practice questions — but learners must verify answers against trusted sources and submit work that reflects their own understanding.
